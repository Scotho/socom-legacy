**Status: Test room only.** Packs are tried in the browser, on your own computer, and kept in that browser's storage.
Nothing you load is uploaded, and nothing reaches a lobby match or another player. Most of what you can see today is
in the **test room** (the game's page at `/testroom`), using the earlier pack formats; a rules pack runs in the
**Forge**'s local Play; and a v2 pack can be checked and stored, but not yet applied. Each section below says which.

You need the game set up in your browser with your own disc, as for playing: a pack changes what the game draws or
does, so the game has to be there first.

## At a glance

| what you want to try | where | status |
|---|---|---|
| an `s2u-mod` data override (replace or add one of the game's files) | the test room's **Mods** panel | Test room only |
| a texture pack for one map | the test room's Experimental view, **Import pack (.zip)** | Test room only |
| an HD texture pack | the same **Import pack (.zip)** button: imported and stored, never drawn | Planned -- held off until the owner lifts the hold |
| a model pack (GLB models in place of the game's meshes) | the test room with `modmodels` in the address | Test room only |
| a v2 rules pack, like [your first pack](/guides/first-pack/) | the Forge's Rules panel or its agent connection, then Play | Forge, local only |
| any v2 pack: is it valid? | the agent connection: the solo test room's **Connect your agent** (outside Play), or the Forge's | Format only: checked and stored, never applied |

The formats themselves are described on [The earlier formats](/kinds/earlier-formats/) and [Pack kinds](/kinds/).

## The test room's Mods panel (`s2u-mod`)

Status: **Test room only.**

Open the test room. Its side panel has a **Mods** section:

1. Turn on **Enable mods**.
2. Press **Add a mod (.zip)** and pick your zip, or **Add a mod folder** and pick the folder. A mod has its `mod.json`
   at the top, or inside the one folder a zipped directory adds.
3. The mod appears as a row. A mod with a problem shows `invalid:` and the reason instead; the other mods still load.
4. Turning a mod on or off, or moving it, does not reload the map. Press **Reload map** when the panel asks.

Mods apply from the top of the list down, so when two mods replace the same file, the lower one wins; the panel names
every conflict and its winner. This browser keeps your mods, their order and their switches until you press
**Remove**.

The panel is in every build of the test room, offline and online. It is not used in a lobby match reached through the
normal menus, and not in the replay viewer: there, the game always reads its own files.

## Texture packs: the Experimental view

Status: **Test room only.**

A texture pack replaces some of one map's solid textures with your own images. In the test room, open the
**Experimental (adaptation)** section and select the Experimental view. Its **Texture pack** row has:

- **Import pack (.zip)**: a base pack zip whose PNGs you replaced at 2x or 4x. It is checked, built for the map and
  kept in this browser only; a refusal says why.
- the **Texture pack** chooser: **Original** (no pack) or a pack from the current map's library;
- **Remove**, to drop the chosen pack.

The base pack is made from your own copy of the game by a separate local tool; this wiki does not cover that step
yet. A texture the pack does not cover, or cannot replace, keeps its original.

## HD texture packs

Status: **Planned** -- held off until the owner lifts the hold (the ui package's `screens/videoPicture.ts`,
`VIDEO_PICTURE_HD_OFFERED`, lines 27-32; the viewer's `experimentalGraphics.ts`, line 437).

The same **Import pack (.zip)** button also takes an HD texture pack (a replacement pack for every texture: world,
characters, weapons, effects, menus). It is recognised by its own layout, checked, and kept in this browser; one HD
pack at a time, so a new import replaces the old one (the viewer's `hdTexturePackServed.ts`, `importZip`). That is as
far as it goes: in every build the **HD texture pack - PCSX2** switch that would turn it on is drawn but disabled, with
the note "Held until after the GPU work", and the HD picture is offered nowhere. You can import and store a pack, but
nothing draws it until the hold is lifted. [The earlier formats](/kinds/earlier-formats/) describes the format.

## Model packs

Status: **Test room only.**

A model pack draws your own GLB models in place of some of the game's meshes. It is off unless the page is the test
room **and** the address contains `modmodels`, for example `/testroom?modmodels`. Then:

1. Open **Settings**. At the bottom is **Model pack**.
2. **Choose model folder**, or **Import model pack (.zip)**. The pack holds a `models.json` and the GLB files it names;
   anything else is ignored.
3. If a map is already on screen, press **Reload map** once the status line says the pack is on.

**Clear model pack** removes it. A model pack changes what is drawn only; what you collide with stays the game's.

## Rules packs: the Forge's Play

Status: **Forge, local only.** The Forge is the game's level editor, at `/forge`. Its work is paused while other
parts of the game come first, so it may change before rules packs reach anywhere else.

A rules pack, such as [your first pack](/guides/first-pack/), runs only in the Forge's own **Play**: an offline match
on the layout you are editing, on your computer. There are two ways to give the Forge your rules:

- **By hand, in the Rules panel.** Open the **Rules** panel (right side, under Edit), press **New rules**, then
  **Add rule** and type each rule as JSON text, the same text as in your rules file. **Check rules** lists any
  refusal with the field it is about.
- **Through an agent.** The Forge's **Agent** tab (**Connect your agent**) lets an AI agent or tool you connect
  yourself work in the editor. Its `rules.import` takes a rules pack's files (`s2u-pack.json` and the rules file) and
  checks them with the same checker, then makes them the layout's rules.

Then choose **Play the layout**. The match runs your rules: in the first pack's case, a message at the start of each
round, and every hit at half damage. With no rules, the layout plays as before.

Rules never travel with a share code, and a layout with rules cannot be played in a lobby: both are refused by name
today.

## A v2 pack's import: checked and stored, not applied

Status: **Format only.**

Any v2 pack, of any kind, can be given to the pack library through an agent connection's `packs.import`, with the
kind `pack`, as a zip or a folder. Two places offer one:

- **The test room.** At the bottom of the solo test room's settings panel, **Connect your agent** lets an AI agent or
  tool you connect yourself bring packs in. It is there in the solo room only (not in an online match or a replay),
  and it imports or removes a pack only while the room is in Build, not during Play (the viewer's
  `agent/testroomInstall.ts`, line 29, and `agent/testroomTarget.ts`, line 23).
- **The Forge.** The Forge's **Agent** tab (**Connect your agent**) does the same in the editor (the viewer's
  `agent/forgePacks.ts`, line 17).

What happens, in either place:

1. The pack is read: `s2u-pack.json` at the top of the zip, or in the one folder a zipped directory adds.
2. It is checked with the same checker as everywhere else: every field, every file's hash and size, every path. A
   pack that fails is refused with a report, each line naming a code from the [Refusal codes](/reference/codes/)
   and the file or field it is about.
3. A pack that passes is stored in this browser and listed with its id, version and kinds.

That is all, for now: nothing in the game applies a v2 pack yet, and asking to apply one is refused. Storing a pack
is still useful as a check: if the library takes it, the pack is valid. Rules packs are the exception above, run
through the Forge's Rules panel rather than from the library.

## When something is refused

Every refusal names a code, and most name the file or field. [Refusal codes](/reference/codes/) lists them all, and
[Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting/) has a fix for each. Your pack never changes the game's own files: remove it,
or reload without it, and the game is as it was.
